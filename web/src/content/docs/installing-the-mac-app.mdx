The Mac app is where Eavesy does its best work — capturing your screen and the
audio your Mac plays, alongside your microphone, with no length limit and files
written straight to your disk.

## Requirements

- **macOS 13 (Ventura) or later**
- Apple silicon (Intel Macs are not supported yet)
- ~150 MB of disk space, plus room for your recordings

## Install with Homebrew

If you use [Homebrew](https://brew.sh), one command installs the app:

```bash
brew install --cask williamrogersdev/eavesy/eavesy
```

Eavesy keeps itself up to date, so you don&rsquo;t need `brew upgrade` for it.
To remove it later, run `brew uninstall --cask eavesy` (add `--zap` to also
delete its settings, caches, and logs; your recordings in `~/Eavesy` are kept).

## Or install the download in three steps

1. **Download** the app from [eavesy.com/download/mac](/download/mac).
2. **Open the `.dmg`** and drag **Eavesy** into your Applications folder.
3. **Launch it** from Applications (or Spotlight).

<Callout type="note" title="First launch">
Because the app is distributed outside the App Store, macOS may ask you to
confirm the first launch. If you see a Gatekeeper prompt, open
**System Settings → Privacy &amp; Security**, scroll to the bottom, and click
**Open Anyway**.
</Callout>

## Grant permissions

To capture a full session, Eavesy needs two macOS permissions. It will prompt
you the first time you record — or you can grant them ahead of time in
**System Settings → Privacy &amp; Security**:

- **Screen &amp; System Audio Recording** — lets Eavesy capture what&rsquo;s on
  screen and the audio your Mac is playing (your call&rsquo;s other side).
- **Microphone** — captures your voice.

<Callout type="privacy">
Permissions are enforced by macOS. Eavesy only captures while a recording is
running. Eavesy does not train models on your content; see
[Privacy &amp; your data](/docs/privacy-and-data) for each processor&rsquo;s policy.
</Callout>

## Set your save location

Open **Settings → Recordings folder** and choose where sessions are written. Point it at a
folder you already sync, or at your [Obsidian](/integrations/obsidian) vault, and
each capture lands there automatically as Markdown, JSON, and audio.
