The Mac app captures three streams at once: the **audio your Mac is playing**
(the other side of a call), your **microphone**, and your **screen**. That&rsquo;s
what makes Eavesy bot-free — nothing has to join the meeting, because everything
is recorded locally on your machine.

## What gets captured

- **System audio** — anything coming out of your speakers: the remote
  participants on a call, a video you&rsquo;re watching, a voice note.
- **Microphone** — your voice.
- **Screen** — what&rsquo;s on display, so the brief reflects what you were
  looking at, not just what was said.

<Callout type="note" title="No bot, no link">
Eavesy never appears in the participant list and needs no meeting link. It works
on [Zoom](/integrations/zoom), [Google Meet](/integrations/google-meet),
[Teams](/integrations/microsoft-teams), and
[in-person conversations](/integrations/in-person) the same way.
</Callout>

## Permissions you&rsquo;ll need

macOS gates screen and audio capture. The first time you record, you&rsquo;ll be
asked to allow:

- **Screen &amp; System Audio Recording**
- **Microphone**

If a recording is silent or screen-only, it&rsquo;s almost always a missing
permission — see [Troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting).

## Getting clean audio

- Use the same **output device** you can hear from — Eavesy captures whatever
  your Mac is playing, so if sound is routed to a device you&rsquo;ve muted, it
  won&rsquo;t be captured.
- For in-person conversations, a quiet room and the built-in mic are plenty;
  make a short test recording before an important session if you are unsure
  about input levels.
- Headphones are fine — system-audio capture happens before the signal reaches
  them, so it&rsquo;s recorded even when nothing plays out loud.

<Callout type="tip">
Eavesy does not currently provide an instant transcript-based level check. Make
a short recording, stop, and confirm the audio and processed transcript before
an important call.
</Callout>
